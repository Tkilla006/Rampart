# 3. Character creation

Creation has nine steps, and two of them do different jobs: **origin** is where and how you grew up (languages, contacts, worldview, community reactions); **background** is what you did before the academy (skills, ingrained Habits, baggage). Combined with academy record, these also decide which training officer you get.

## Step 1: Identity

- Name, gender, pronouns, appearance, age (21 to 26 for a young rookie).
- Heritage and languages. Spanish, Korean, Armenian, Tagalog, Russian and others unlock witnesses, informants and dialogue nobody else on the squad can reach. Heritage shapes how some civilians, suspects and coworkers react, in ways true to 2010 LA; the game shows reactions without judging the player for them.

## Step 2: Origin

| Origin | Gives | Story hooks |
|---|---|---|
| LA native (South LA, Boyle Heights, Koreatown, the Valley, Westside) | Streetwise bonus and contacts in that neighborhood | Posted near people you grew up with, some now on the wrong side |
| Small-town transplant (Midwest, South, Texas) | Adaptability; no local contacts | Culture shock at LA and cape culture |
| Big-city transplant (New York, Chicago) | Street sense, cynicism | An outsider who thinks they know cities |
| First-generation American (Mexican, Korean, Armenian, Filipino, Salvadoran families) | Native language, community trust in one group | Family expectations; relatives back home in proxy-war zones |
| Child of Eastern-bloc defectors | Russian or another bloc language | Extra background scrutiny; the handler agency and possibly the KGB watch you |
| Cascade-displaced | Survivor's resilience | Your family lost everything in 1996–2000 and resettled in LA |

## Step 3: Background

Every background has an Edge, one or more Habits, Baggage, and an Unlearning path (section 5).

| Background | Edge | Habits | Baggage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Military: combat veteran | Most lethal rookie; discipline, cover, room clearing | Engage the Threat, Dominate, military report language | Combat trauma track and deployment triggers |
| Military: medic | Triage, calm with gore | Run to the Wounded | Trauma from the dead |
| Military: MP | Detentions, searches, procedure head start | Dominate | Quick to go hands-on |
| Military: non-combat | Discipline, fitness, command presence | Cover First | None |
| College graduate | Report writing, law, interviewing; promotion track | Book Answer (hesitation) | Student loans; seen as soft |
| EMT or firefighter | Triage, scene management | Run to the Wounded | Existing trauma from past calls |
| Corrections or jail deputy | Handling violent people, contraband, gangs | Control the Cell | Hardened; jail reputation |
| Private security or bouncer | Defensive tactics, crowds, nightlife contacts | Hands On | Old associates in shady places |
| Cape industry (PR staffer, cape network stringer, rubble crew) | Ledger knowledge, media contacts, PR tricks | Find the Camera | Old employers want favors; squad thinks you're a plant |
| Trades or blue-collar | Fitness, mechanics, rapport with working people | Blunt | Family to support; wear and tear |
| Cadet program | Department culture and contacts | Book Answer | Naive; hardest fall when disillusioned |

**Military deployments** (pick one): Central America (advising government forces against Cuban-backed guerrillas), southern Africa (covert support beside Rhodesian units; unspeakable), the Korean DMZ (garrison and infiltrators), West Berlin (spy games, little shooting). Then pick role and whether you saw combat.

## Step 4: Where you were during the Cascade

- **Saved by a hero:** starts as a fan; disillusionment arc.
- **Lost someone to a fight:** bonus investigating heroes, penalty with hero PR teams.
- **A sibling manifested:** a family secret or a registration fight; major personal storyline.
- **Grew up on Ledger culture:** hero data and gossip as a real investigative skill.
- **Anti-cape household:** family ties to anti-super movements that later become a case.

## Step 5: Why you joined

Serve the community, family legacy, steady paycheck and pension, get out of the neighborhood, or get close to the capes. Motivation sets what restores your stress and which endings feel earned.

## Step 6: Academy record

- **Class standing:** top of class (high expectations), middle, or barely passed (everyone waits for you to fail).
- **Academy strength:** range, physical training, report writing, Spanish, or defensive tactics.
- **A note in your file (optional):** a complaint, injury or covered-up incident; extra story options at a reputation cost.

## Step 7: Attributes, skills, traits and flaws

Spend attribute points and receive skill packages (section 4). Pick two traits (Steady Hands, Silver Tongue, Night Owl, Iron Stomach, Bilingual Bonus, Gym Rat) and one flaw (Temper, Debt, Bad Knee, Family Obligation, Bottle Problem, Big Mouth, Can't Lie).

## Step 8: Personal life at the start

- **Housing:** parents' place (cheap, no privacy), Koreatown apartment with a roommate, or Valley studio (long commute).
- **Relationship:** single, dating, engaged or married; optionally a young child from a past relationship with a custody schedule.
- **Money:** loans, family you support, or a small cushion.
- **Car:** a beater that breaks down, or a nicer car financed beyond your means.

## Step 9: Secret (optional, hard mode)

Something kept out of your background check: past drug use, a cousin in a gang, a debt to the wrong people, or a latent Tier 1 power you've never told anyone about. An unregistered superhuman cop is a felony waiting to be discovered.

## How creation sets the game

| Choice | Decides |
|---|---|
| Origin | Languages, informants, community reputation start, family cast |
| Background | Skills, Habits, trauma, unique combat actions |
| Academy record + background | Which FTO you get, and therefore your call mix and early playstyle |
| Cascade history | Attitude toward capes, trauma triggers in cape incidents |
| Personal start | Starting stress, money, time pressure |
| Secret | A story time bomb |

Example combinations: a Boyle Heights native who fought in Central America speaks the language of the people his war displaced; a Koreatown kid who did DMZ duty finds North Korean defector networks in his own neighborhood.
