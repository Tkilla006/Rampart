# 5. Habits, stress and trauma

Habits are trained reflexes that fire under stress unless you fight them; trauma is a hidden track that changes how your character perceives, sleeps, loves and shoots. Together they make a veteran play differently from a college graduate, not just with bigger numbers.

## Composure

Composure = Nerve × 3. It drops from gunfire, gore, cape incidents, trauma triggers, screaming civilians, and fatigue; it recovers between calls, at Code 7, and through coping off duty.

| Composure | Effect |
|---|---|
| Full | Normal |
| Below half | −10 to Intellect and Empathy skills; tunnel vision begins |
| Below a quarter | −20 to Intellect and Empathy, −10 to Firearms; Habits fire automatically |
| Zero | Freeze, flee or fire; the game picks from your Habits and trauma |

Fine motor skills and complex thinking collapse first; drilled responses survive. Stress is shown, never numbered: tunnel vision (a dark vignette that hides threats until you spend AP to scan), auditory exclusion (muffled sound, a harder-to-hear radio), and time distortion (your animations slow, the danger doesn't).

## Habits

Each Habit has a trigger, a default action, and a strength from 1 to 3. Strength rises each time you act on it and falls when you override it or retrain it with your FTO.

| Composure | How the Habit behaves |
|---|---|
| Above half | Shown as a highlighted "instinct" option; every choice is free |
| Half to a quarter | The instinct is free; any other option needs an Override check |
| Below a quarter | The Habit fires automatically unless you pass an Override check at a penalty |
| Surprise | Fires on your first action regardless of Composure |

```
Override = (Nerve × 5) + (relevant skill × 10) − (Habit strength × 10) − stress penalties
```

A successful override costs 1 AP and some Composure: fighting your own reflexes is exhausting.

| Habit | Backgrounds | Trigger | Default action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engage the Threat | Combat veteran | A weapon is seen | Draw and fire a controlled pair |
| Dominate | Military, corrections | Non-compliance | Escalate to hands-on or force |
| Cover First | Military | Gunfire or ambush | Move to hard cover (this one helps) |
| Run to the Wounded | Medic, EMT, firefighter | A casualty is visible | Move to them, even across open ground |
| Book Answer | Cadet program, college graduate | A situation not in training | Hesitate a turn, look for procedure |
| Control the Cell | Corrections | A suspect in custody | Restraint over medical care |
| Hands On | Bouncer, security | Contact range | Close distance too fast |
| Find the Camera | Cape industry | A cape incident | Assess the media angle before the threat |
| Military Language | Military | Writing a report | Combat wording offered by default |

**Unlearning:** over probation your FTO can grind military or corrections habits into police habits, or reinforce them. Some FTOs want a shooter.

## The trauma track

Each critical incident (a shooting, a dead child, a cape massacre, a partner wounded, a body you can't forget) adds trauma points and tags a new trigger drawn from it. Combat veterans start with points and deployment triggers. Every character can develop trauma; it's the job.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Hypervigilance | +10 Perception, ambush resistance, worse sleep |
| Intrusions | Flashbacks during triggers |
| Startle | Overreaction to backfires, fireworks, slamming doors |
| Numbing | Empathy penalties; relationships cool |
| Avoidance | You start dodging certain calls, places, people |
| Irritability | Sharper dialogue forced at home; the gentle option may vanish |

## Triggers and episodes

A trigger in play calls for a Nerve check. On a failure, an episode begins:

- **Flashback overlay:** the palette shifts to your deployment or an old scene for a turn or two; you act on what you think you see.
- **Misperception:** a phone, wallet or toy briefly reads as a gun. The most dangerous episode, and an honest picture of how some real shootings happen.
- **Freeze:** you lose your next turn.
- **Fight:** a Habit fires at maximum strength.

Off duty: nightmares (a Composure penalty next shift), snapping at a partner or child, gripping the wheel at a backfire, and the bottle calling.

Deployment-specific triggers include burning smells, crowded markets, helicopters overhead, and, most dangerous in this world, armed teenagers. A veteran of a war fought with child soldiers may freeze or fire when a 15-year-old pulls a gun.

## Coping and recovery

| Option | Effect | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Department psychologist | Weakens triggers over time | Stigma with some of the squad; noted in your file |
| Peer support or partner | Composure recovery, relationship gains | Requires opening up; some will judge |
| Spouse or family | Strong recovery if the relationship is healthy | Risk of bringing the job home |
| Exercise and sleep | Slow, steady recovery | Off-duty time |
| Faith or confession | Moral injury relief | Only if it fits the character |
| Alcohol or pills | Instant Composure relief | Dependence, impaired shifts, its own crisis |
| Ignoring it | Nothing | The track keeps growing |

Recovery never erases a trigger; it weakens it. A well-supported character handles triggers in the late game; a neglected one is one bad call from disaster.

## Fatigue

Long shifts, overtime, court on days off, and broken sleep build Fatigue, which lowers maximum Composure and Perception. Push too long and you miss things on calls.
