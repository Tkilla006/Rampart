# 8. Shifts, calls and playthrough styles

A shift plays like an episode: station, calls of uneven intensity, paperwork, home. Dispatch and your training officer decide most of what you face, so your FTO is the biggest single influence on what kind of game you play, and your control grows as you earn trust.

## Shift structure

Patrol works compressed schedules (a few long shifts a week), giving a rhythm of hard days and days off for the personal game.

1. **Arrival:** locker room, gossip, who looks hungover.
2. **Kit room:** shotgun, Taser, radio; the kit room officer's approval decides quality.
3. **Roll call:** crime trends, wanted suspects, cape alerts, BOLOs; information that matters later; pranks.
4. **Car check:** inspect the car and search the back seat for what the last shift left; skipping it can mean planted contraband later.
5. **Patrol:** calls, self-initiated activity, quiet stretches.
6. **Code 7:** meal with your partner; companion conversations; Composure restore.
7. **End of watch:** booking, evidence, reports (section 9).
8. **Evaluation:** FTO grades the shift.
9. **Overtime or home:** a late arrest means pay and lost sleep; then the personal game.

## Radio and call format

- **Call sign:** Rampart is Area 2, so you are something like 2A43 (Area 2, two-officer car, beat 43).
- **Response codes:** Code 2 (urgent, no lights), Code 3 (lights and siren), Code 4 (no further help needed), Code 6 (out on foot investigating), Code 7 (meal).
- **Call types:** 211 robbery, 245 assault with a deadly weapon, 415 disturbance, 459 burglary, 5150 mental health hold, 10851 stolen vehicle, 23152 DUI, and "officer needs help".
- **Dispatcher Nine** and your Radio and Dispatch skill decide how fast and clear your information is.

## Where calls come from

- **Procedural calls,** weighted by district, watch, calendar (paydays, holidays, heat, the Fourth of July), world events (a Hollywood cape fight sends debris calls into Rampart; a Spark shipment brings an overdose wave), and your reputation.
- **Episode beats:** one handcrafted beat per shift advancing a character or case arc.
- **Personal calls:** your friend's garage, your neighbor's son, your sibling.
- **Signature calls:** rare written set pieces players remember.

## Anatomy of a call

1. **Dispatch information is often wrong.** A hidden reliability roll: a "415 family dispute" is a stabbing; a "man with a gun" has a phone. Perception and Streetwise give hints on approach.
2. **Approach:** Code 2 or 3, park close or out of sight, wait for backup or go in.
3. **Contact:** dialogue, observation, judgment; Critical Time only if it turns dangerous.
4. **Resolution:**

   | Outcome | Time | Consequences |
   |---|---|---|
   | Arrest | Hours (transport, booking, reports) | Stats, court dates, overtime, community effects |
   | Citation | Short | Numbers and resentment |
   | Warning | Very short | Trust, unless they go on to hurt someone |
   | 5150 hold | Long (hospital waits) | Saves lives, ruins your shift |
   | Referral | Short | Community officer approval; rarely sticks |
   | Report only | Medium | Paperwork without justice |
   | Walk away | None | Your FTO notices |

5. **Aftermath:** subpoenas, complaints, a victim who calls you personally, a suspect who remembers your face.

## The pending queue

Rampart is too busy for every call. Pending calls stack up; unanswered ones escalate (a disturbance becomes an assault); the unit that takes the call you skipped sometimes gets hurt. The sergeant wants calls cleared fast, the community wants you to take your time, and your FTO judges which you chose.

## Self-initiated activity

Traffic stops, field interviews and known-spot checks in quiet stretches. Every stop needs reasonable suspicion (Law and Policy checks), stops in some areas raise profiling questions under the consent decree, and some stops find a gun while others leave a resident humiliated.

## Cape incidents in the call system

Ledger alerts in three tiers: debris and aftermath (common), active perimeter (occasional), inside the fight (rare). When the handler agency arrives, the scene is theirs; you can argue, comply, or quietly keep evidence.

## Downtime in the car

Between calls the patrol car is the Baldur's Gate camp: conversations that build approval and reveal backstory and secrets, interrupted by the radio at the worst moments. Code 7 has Rampart flavor: taco trucks on Alvarado, the deli near MacArthur Park, a Filipino bakery that always gives cops extra.

## Who controls the calls

| Phase | Control |
|---|---|
| Field training (about 24 weeks) | Dispatch assigns, your FTO decides what to jump; you can volunteer, push back, or stay silent. Influence = FTO approval + Presence. A disliked rookie gets ignored or punished with worse calls |
| Final probation | You ride with other partners and have far more say; your FTO has already shaped your habits and reputation |

**FTO call selection:** Ortega picks teaching calls (a balanced mix, heavy on procedure); Whitfield jumps every hot call (shots fired, pursuits, help calls, cape perimeters); Arakawa takes community calls and stalls on certain ones, a pattern an alert player will notice. Because academy record and background decide your FTO, character creation quietly sets your playthrough style.

## Playthrough styles

Styles emerge from behavior; the game tracks it and responds.

| Style | How | Rewards | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot-call chaser | Volunteer for everything, push your FTO toward action | Fast skills, squad respect, gang unit recruitment, Metro/SWAT later | Combat after combat, trauma, investigations, injury, real chance of early death |
| Coaster | Avoid hot calls, linger on easy ones, long Code 7s | Less trauma, more personal-life energy | Poor evaluations, extended training, squad contempt, possible washout; the story finds you anyway |
| Community cop | Prioritize residents, follow up, work with the senior lead officer | Neighborhood trust, informants, unique story paths | Slow numbers, a sergeant who thinks you're soft |
| Numbers cop | Stops, arrests, citations | Command likes your stats; promotion paths | Community anger, profiling complaints, the wrong stop on the wrong night |
| Investigator | Evidence, leads, impress the detectives | Main casework access, detective track | Pending calls left to others; powerful enemies |

Two players with identical creation choices get different games: a Whitfield run plays like a war story, an Arakawa run like a slow betrayal, an Ortega run like a procedural that turns into a conspiracy.

## Death and serious injury

| Mode | Rule |
|---|---|
| Standard | Fatal situations usually become critical wounds: survival with lasting damage, months of recovery, possible medical retirement |
| Ironman (End of Watch) | Death is permanent; an epilogue shows the funeral, your FTO and classmates, your family, the squad going back on shift without you, and the fatal call once more through someone else's eyes |

## FTO evaluation and probation

Each shift is graded on officer safety, procedure, report quality, judgment, communication and attitude. Poor evaluations extend field training; persistently poor ones send you to a review that can end your probation.

## Example shift: PM watch, a Saturday in July

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 14:30 | Roll call: Spark dealer BOLO, heat warning; Ducks has filled your locker with Ledger trading cards |
| 15:40 | 415 at a MacArthur Park stall: a vendor turf dispute, solved with Spanish and patience |
| 17:15 | Self-initiated stop on Alvarado: an unlicensed, nervous teenager; cite, warn or search? |
| 18:30 | Code 7 at a taco truck; your FTO starts a story from 1998 and stops |
| 19:50 | Ledger alert: Hollywood fight debris across Echo Park; a dead man in a car; a hero's PR rep arrives before the coroner |
| 22:10 | 245 in Pico-Union: a stabbing, an arrest, your shift's end missed |
| 01:30 | Booking, evidence, reports; the hero's lawyer leaves a voicemail |
| 02:45 | Evaluation, then the drive home |
