# 2. Setting: Los Angeles, 2010

The game takes place in 2010 in the Ledger & Accords world: the Cold War never ended, superhumans are treated like nuclear weapons, and the West scores its heroes in lives saved and lost. Everyday police and civilian technology is accurate to real 2010; the world's advanced technology stays in federal, military and hero hands.

## Where 2010 sits in the world's history

| Year | Event | What it means for a rookie in 2010 |
|---|---|---|
| 1945 | The Erasure: Meridian destroys a Japanese city | Meridian films, memorial anniversaries and protests are part of the calendar |
| 1947 | The Lucerne Accords | Strategic assets are state secrets; the federal handler agency outranks everyone at a scene |
| 1996 | The Cascade: thousands manifest powers | A 22-year-old rookie was about 8; many recruits lived through the chaotic years |
| 1996–2000 | The chaotic years | Ghost districts like the Hole date from this period |
| 2000 | The Ledger launches | Ten years old, still controversial; police statements feed review boards |
| 2000s | Spark, boosters and other superhuman drugs spread | A street-level problem; overdoses and boosted suspects |
| 2010 | The present | Ledger alerts reach flip phones; cape networks dominate cable news |

## The world layer in daily police work

- **The Ledger:** Licensed heroes have public scores (Saved, Lost, Net Lives). Review boards assign each death to a hero, a villain or "unavoidable". Police reports and testimony are evidence. Heroes, PR firms and lawyers lean on cops to shape those reports.
- **The Accords and the handler agency:** A federal agency recruits, manages and covers for heroes, and can take any superhuman scene from local police. Agents are polite, powerful and unaccountable to the LAPD.
- **Power tiers:** Gifted (1), Street (2), City (3), Regional (4), Strategic (5). Patrol officers can sometimes handle Tier 1–2 with conventional tools and must survive, not fight, Tier 3 and above.
- **The rubble economy:** Cleanup corporations bid on impact zones, insurers deny claims with "act of superhuman" exclusions, and ruined blocks become ghost districts.
- **Media:** Cape networks, the Ledger desk, civilian fight footage, hero influencers, biopics and betting markets. Footage is evidence, entertainment and currency at once.
- **The Cold War:** Eastern-bloc agents, defectors, and veterans of the proxy wars (Central America, southern Africa, the Korean DMZ) pass through LA.

## The real LAPD in 2010

| Area | 2010 reality used in the game |
|---|---|
| Oversight | The department is still under the federal consent decree imposed in 2001 after the Rampart scandal; it was lifted in 2013 |
| Ranks | Police Officer I (probationary) advances to Police Officer II after 18 months; training officers are Police Officer III |
| Academy | About six months (28 weeks) at Elysian Park |
| Field training | About 24 weeks with a training officer, extendable if performance is unsatisfactory, then a final probationary period |
| Geography | 21 geographic Areas in four bureaus; Rampart is Area 2 in Central Bureau |
| Video | No body cameras. Digital in-car video reached only South Bureau in 2010, so Rampart cars have no dashcams |
| Sidearm | Glock 22 (.40) standard since 2002; Beretta 92F/FS still approved for veterans |
| Patrol car | Ford Crown Victoria black-and-white with an in-car computer |
| Less-lethal | Taser X26, beanbag shotgun, OC spray, baton |
| Long guns | Remington 870 shotgun from the kit room; patrol rifles require certification rookies do not have |
| Phones | Flip phones and BlackBerrys; the iPhone 4 arrives mid-2010 |

Real-world officials (the chief and mayor in 2010) are replaced by fictional stand-ins. Real gangs inspire, but are not used as, the game's fictional gangs.

## The civilian world

2010 Los Angeles: the recession, rising rents in Echo Park and Silver Lake, MySpace giving way to Facebook, YouTube clips of cape fights, Lakers basketball, and a city that treats superhuman disasters like weather.
