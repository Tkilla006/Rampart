# 10. Court testimony

Court is where your reports come back to test you: tired, nervous, picked apart by someone paid to make you look like a liar, with everything you wrote weeks ago on the table.

## Getting there

- **Subpoenas** arrive in your station box, often for a day off or the morning after an overnight shift. Court pays overtime and costs sleep and family time.
- **On call:** some days you wait in a hallway and are never called; some cases are continued to next month.
- **Preparation:** rereading your report is normal and legal. You may get a short prep session with an overworked prosecutor.

## Hearing types

| Hearing | At stake | Who attacks you |
|---|---|---|
| Suppression hearing | Whether the stop or search was legal; evidence thrown out if not | Defense attorney |
| Preliminary hearing | Enough evidence for trial | Defense attorney |
| Jury trial | Guilt or innocence; rare, high stakes | Defense attorney, before a jury |
| Ledger review board (world) | Which hero or villain is blamed for a death | The hero's lawyers and PR team |
| Board of Rights | Your own discipline | The department advocate |
| Civil deposition | Lawsuits against you or the city | Plaintiff's attorney |

## Structure and meters

1. **Direct:** the prosecutor walks you through your account; you can still trip on your own report.
2. **Cross-examination:** the core of the minigame.
3. **Redirect:** the prosecutor repairs damage.
4. **Ruling:** judge or jury.

**Meters:** Credibility (visible; how believable you are right now), Composure (low Composure brings back Habits, temper and military language), Case strength (how much depends on you, and how it is holding).

## Cross-examination attacks

| Attack | Example | Targets |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | "Your report says 22:14; your partner's says 22:30." | Consistency flags |
| Boilerplate | "Describe exactly what 'furtive movement' means." | Vague wording |
| Memory | "How many shots did you fire? Are you sure?" | Fuzzy and distorted cards |
| Procedure | "Did you read him his rights before or after he talked?" | Missing elements |
| Experience | "How many weeks have you been a police officer?" | Being a rookie |
| Bias | "You have personal feelings about this neighborhood?" | Origin, complaints, relationships |
| History | "Have you been the subject of complaints?" | IA record |
| Provocation | Mockery designed to make you angry | Temper, Habits, trauma |
| The exhibit | "I'd like to play a bystander's video." | Your report against footage |

## Responses

| Response | Effect |
|---|---|
| Precise answer | Strong if the memory card is clear and the report precise; raises Credibility |
| "I don't recall" | Honest; small Credibility cost, no new contradiction; evasive if overused |
| "May I refer to my report?" | Legal and common; safe if the report is good, exposing if it is weak |
| Concede the point | Admitting small mistakes builds trust |
| Deflect | Presence check; fails if pushed |
| Lie | Deception against the attorney and the evidence; caught = perjury, IA, possible charges |
| Lose control | Sometimes forced at low Composure; Credibility collapses |

A good prosecutor objects often to protect you; a burned-out one barely notices.

## Courtroom cast

- **DDA Monica Feld:** overworked, competent; fights for your cases if she trusts your work, stops filing them if you burn her once.
- **Elliot Shaw:** public defender and possible boyfriend; he'll take you apart if your report deserves it, then you both go home.
- **Hal Brenner:** expensive private defense for the gang unit, the bookmaker and Castellan's interests; calm, surgical, very well informed about you.
- **Gregory Vance:** Castellan's counsel at Ledger boards; treats police testimony as a PR problem.
- **Judges:** a strict former prosecutor, a sympathetic veteran, and one who distrusts every LAPD officer because of the scandal years.

## Presentation

A side-view pixel courtroom. The gallery matters: the defendant's family, gang members staring to intimidate, a reporter, a grief network organizer, your FTO, or nobody. Stress shows on your character: sweat, fidgeting, glances at the clock.

## Consequences

Convictions, dismissals and suppressed evidence send people back into your district (guilty and innocent). Credibility moves toward or away from the Brady list. Feld's approval decides whether your arrests get filed. A caught lie brings perjury and the Board of Rights. Court days eat your days off; watching Elliot cross-examine you can change your relationship.

## Example: Pico-Union gun stop, suppression hearing

Your report: "Suspect made a furtive movement toward waistband. Officer conducted a pat-down for weapons."

- **"What does 'furtive movement' mean?"** Precise answer (if the card is clear, Report Writing 55%), refer to the report (safe, weak), or "I don't recall exactly" (honest; the search looks less justified).
- **"You stopped him because he was a young Latino man on a gang corner?"** Concede part ("The area was part of it; his behavior was the rest"), deflect (Presence 40%), or lose control at low Composure with the Temper flaw (Credibility drops; Feld puts her face in her hands).
- **The exhibit:** a neighbor's phone video shows him standing still for 30 seconds. If your card was distorted, your report is provably wrong: the gun is suppressed, the case dismissed, a credibility flag added. That night Elliot asks whether you lied or were wrong, and you honestly don't know.
